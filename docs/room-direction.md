# Living room direction

The living room should read at first glance as a dark library someone lives
in. The books, the record player, and the writing desk must still work. This
page records the look we are aiming for, how close the room is, and where the
next effort should go.

## The look

Two reference images, chosen on September 22, set the mood:

- dark green walls and brown oak joinery;
- oxblood or worn-leather seating;
- a dense Persian rug;
- warm lamps that make small pools of light; and
- cool light from the window.

Keep the isometric cutaway and all three interactive objects. Don't chase
detail that disappears at the size the room shows on the page, or that would
push the GLBs past the 11 MB budget.

## Where the room stands

The September 22 pass matched the references' composition and mood. It
added oxblood seating, a leather desk chair, and a round marble-topped table.
It darkened the oak, added parquet, green walls, and a near-black surround,
and filled the shelves with greenery. Burgundy curtains hang on a brass rod.
The light pools are smaller, the corners are deeper, and a closer 4:3 view
fills the frame.

## What to do next

The composition is right. Material richness is what the room still lacks. If
another pass is worth doing, add broad surface variation to the leather, wood,
and rug, the kind that survives baking. Don't add small props or larger
textures unless a camera render shows a clear gain.

Change one family of materials per pass, and bake only the groups it touches.
Keep the pass if `bun run room:check chromium --shots` shows an improvement at
desktop and laptop sizes.
